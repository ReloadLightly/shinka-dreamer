# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 4 remains the best observed design at 0.950906 fitness:** 69/72 escapes, zero deaths, three timeouts, and 65.78 steps per successful escape. Its uniform/slow/fast motion mixture, anonymous transition inference, explicit collision hazards, and three-step planning remain the strongest combined pattern. Generations 18–20 nearly matched it at **0.9508**, reproducing the same escape and safety results with slightly longer successful trajectories (**66.23 steps**).
- **Attempted-motion learning with blocked moves and observation ambiguity remains a recurring component of strong agents.** This extends the earlier improvement from Generation 0’s occupancy learner (**0.799**) to Generation 1’s adaptive predictor (**0.917**). Generations 18–20 retain this modeling approach, but the comparisons do not isolate its contribution from navigation and collision avoidance.
- **Generation 24’s censored-motion learner and survival beam planner produced the strongest newer speed–reliability tradeoff:** **0.9419 fitness**, 68/72 escapes, no timeouts, and **57.74 steps per escape**. It exceeded Generation 21’s **0.9358** with the same aggregate escape/death counts and substantially shorter successful trajectories. Nevertheless, its four deaths left it below Generation 4.
- **Recovery mechanisms show mixed, design-dependent associations.** Earlier stall recovery accompanied improvement from Generation 8’s **0.8598** to Generation 9’s **0.8910**. In the newer results, targeted observation in Generation 19 and surprise-triggered restarting in Generation 20 produced no change in the reported outcome aggregates relative to Generation 18; their separate benefits remain unestablished.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Arrival-aware and survival-conditioned planning often exchanged timeouts for deaths.** Generation 21 achieved **0.9358**, with 68 escapes, four deaths, and no timeouts, versus Generation 4’s 69 escapes, zero deaths, and three timeouts. Generation 25 reproduced essentially the same outcomes at **0.936**. Eliminating timeouts did not improve overall fitness.
- **Additional adaptation or recovery machinery did not consistently improve completed designs.** Generation 22’s information-clocked adaptation scored **0.9147**, with approximately five deaths and one timeout. Generation 26’s bounded safe detours scored **0.9251**, with four deaths and one timeout. Both were below Generation 21, although the supplied summaries cannot establish which implementation differences caused the losses.
- **Deeper survival-conditioned lookahead remains an inconsistent pattern.** Generation 27’s two-option third-step beam scored **0.9248**, with 67/72 escapes and **66.46 steps per escape**—both worse than Generation 4. This reinforces the earlier weak results of Generation 6 (**0.7802**) and Generation 12 (**0.9066**), while Generation 24 shows that some beam-based combinations perform substantially better.
- **Better prediction diagnostics have not established better control.** Generations 21–27 generally beat their supplied Brier baselines while remaining below the best task score. The earlier Generation 6 achieved near-cell Brier **0.00293** despite low fitness. Sparse occupancy, different visited states, and absent intervention comparisons prevent treating these losses as evidence that added learning mechanisms improved escape performance.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program models attempted movement rather than equating displacement with intent.** `_terrain_destination` maps blocked and corner-blocked attempts back to their origin. `_infer_anonymous` marginalizes assignments whose occupied-cell union matches observations, allowing overlaps. `_learn` discounts directionally uninformative posteriors and skips transitions crossing scheduled terrain-change boundaries. These mechanisms address concrete inference ambiguities within the strongest evaluated design.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add observation goals for stale connectivity bottlenecks.** When a remembered door or exit is unreachable, identify old wall and diagonal-corner cells whose reopening could reconnect the route, then select reachable viewpoints that reveal them. Preserve the current hazard planner and activate this only after failed progress: all episodes collected two keys, making targeted route recovery more promising than broad exploration bonuses.

2. **Continue safety lookahead beyond intermediate targets.** In `value`, replace the unconditional zero return at a reached target with a short continuation that checks safe onward movement for keys, doors, and frontiers; retain terminal treatment for an actual escape. This tests whether target truncation favors hazardous dead ends while preserving the successful three-step horizon and immediate occupied-cell exclusion.

3. **Maintain a filtered belief over recently hidden enemies.** Carry forward enemy probability mass between observations, propagate it through blocked-attempt kernels, and remove mass from visibly empty cells; use diffuse density for unresolved enemy mass without double-counting tracked hypotheses. The current `_forecast` rebuilds hidden intensity each turn, so this extends the winning predictor with temporal memory that could distinguish recently vacated corridors from plausible incoming threats.

4. **Preserve expert identity through multistep forecasts.** Propagate the uniform, slow, and fast movement laws separately across all three forecast steps, compute occupancy and collision hazards within each expert, and combine the resulting predictions using the learned weights. Keep the existing uniform probability floor and horizon-one behavior initially: this isolates whether mixing laws before every propagation loses useful temporal structure, without adding the deeper search associated with inconsistent results.

5. **Make door transitions explicit in planning branches.** Replace `_forecast`’s assumed opening of every adjacent door with action-dependent door state, carried through movement legality, enemy kernels, and planner memoization according to interaction timing. Also store the preceding door state so `_learn` can handle opening transitions separately from ordinary movement evidence; this extends the best program’s successful blocked-attempt modeling to a concrete source of transition ambiguity.
