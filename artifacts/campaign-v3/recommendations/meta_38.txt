**Program Name: Online Occupancy Learning with Risk-Aware Dijkstra Planning**
- **Implementation**: Accumulates terrain and learns horizon-one enemy occupancy probabilities from anonymous local features using prior-smoothed counts, without forgetting older observations. Eight-direction Dijkstra planning combines movement, predicted risk and revisit costs, prioritizes keys and exit/door goals, explores frontiers, and waits when the next move is substantially riskier.
- **Performance**: Passed all validation tests with task score 0.799 across 72 development episodes, 77.8% escape, 20.8% death, 1.875 keys per episode, and 54.34 steps per successful escape.
- **Feedback**: Occupancy Brier losses modestly outperform supplied audit baselines, but sparse occupancy and absent comparative control results prevent attributing escape performance to learning. Stationary-regime escape is weakest at 70.8%; cumulative updates lack an explicit adaptation mechanism for switching dynamics, and only 14 switch episodes actually encountered a switch.

- **Generation 37’s belief-route controller is the strongest new variant:** 0.9385 fitness, 68/72 escapes, one death, three timeouts, and 60.62 steps per escape. Generation 29’s survival-conditioned observation planner follows at 0.9375 with the same escape count, two deaths, two timeouts, and 64.9 steps per escape. Both combine anonymous attempted-motion inference with uniform/slow/fast experts and risk-aware routing; their summaries do not isolate which differences explain the ranking.
- **Generation 24 retains the strongest previously observed faster alternative:** 0.9419 fitness, 68 escapes, four deaths, zero timeouts, and 57.74 steps per escape. Its censored-motion learner and survival beam planner remain above all new variants in fitness, although below the current best in escape reliability and safety.
- **Attempted-motion modeling remains common to strong programs.** The historical improvement from Generation 0’s occupancy learner, at 0.799, to Generation 1’s adaptive predictor, at 0.917, is consistent with this pattern. Current high performers explicitly account for blocked attempts and ambiguous enemy identities, but concurrent controller changes prevent attributing those gains solely to learning.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Survival-conditioned and deeper planning have not consistently matched the best controller’s safety.** Generations 28 and 32 scored 0.903 and 0.9066, respectively, with 65/72 escapes and approximately five and six deaths. Generation 31’s survival-aware routing and recovery waypoints scored 0.8923 with 64 escapes and six deaths. These results extend the earlier mixed findings for Generations 21–27: fewer timeouts or shorter successful trajectories did not reliably compensate for deaths.
- **Persistent hidden-enemy filtering did not yield stronger overall outcomes in the reported variants.** Generation 33 scored 0.9148 and Generation 36 scored 0.9200; both escaped in 66/72 episodes. Their 100% switching escape rates coincided with only 79.2% stationary escape, versus the current best’s 91.7%. This identifies the observed weakness without establishing that filtering caused it.
- **Additional observation machinery sometimes produced unchanged or nearly unchanged results.** Generations 32 and 34 both report 0.9066 fitness, 90.3% escape, and 57.1 steps per escape despite their different door-state and stale-bottleneck descriptions. Generations 30 and 35 likewise report essentially unchanged fitness, approximately 0.9269–0.9270, and identical outcome rates. These extend the earlier absence of aggregate outcome gains from Generations 19–20’s additions.
- **Lower prediction loss remains insufficient evidence of better control.** Generation 29’s near-cell Brier loss, 0.00751, is slightly below the best program’s 0.007607, yet its fitness and escape count are lower. The historical Generation 6 result—0.00293 near-cell Brier but 0.7802 fitness—makes the same limitation especially clear. ## Implementation Insights

- **The best learner distinguishes attempted direction from realized displacement.** `_terrain_destination` maps blocked and corner-blocked attempts back to their origins. `_infer_anonymous` sums assignments whose occupied-cell union matches observations, permitting overlapping enemies. `_learn` skips scheduled terrain-change boundaries and scales count updates by posterior departure from the prior movement law, limiting concentration from directionally uninformative observations.
- **Its adaptation maintains multiple memory timescales and persistent probability support.** Slow and fast excess counts retain 0.985 and 0.86 per enabled update call; discounted observation likelihoods adjust expert weights. `_combined_law` reserves 12% uniform mass. This persistent ensemble accompanies 23/24 switching escapes without the surprise-triggered rebuilding described for Generations 30 and 35, which achieved the same switching escape rate but lower overall fitness.
- **Its planner distinguishes occupancy predictions from collision hazards.** `_forecast` combines enemy presence before and after movement, subtracting stay overlap. The controller excludes currently occupied immediate destinations and penalizes `-log(1-hazard)` with horizon weights 1.0, 0.55, and 0.25. These concrete safeguards accompany zero observed deaths; the supplied comparisons do not establish their individual causal contributions.
- **Its spatial representation supports navigation without a persistent hidden-enemy filter.** Localization follows reported displacement, terrain observations retain timestamps, and hidden-enemy intensity is rebuilt during forecasting. Exploration rewards unseen cells, with an 18% tolerance for retaining an existing frontier target. Coverage of 69.29%, reconstruction of 68.42%, and stale-cell reconstruction of 98.48% indicate that unseen terrain accounts for much of the aggregate reconstruction deficit.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Use arrival-time risk for route selection.** `_distances` currently charges horizon-one occupancy along the entire route, potentially treating a temporary enemy position as a lasting obstacle. Test time-binned routing that propagates the existing attempted-motion mixture to approximate arrival times, while preserving immediate occupied-cell exclusion and the conservative three-step collision planner; Generation 37’s strong belief-route result makes this a promising direction.

2. **Add a progress-triggered alternative-route controller.** Track repeated position–target cycles and lack of improvement in distance to the objective, then select a reachable waypoint that approaches the same objective through another corridor. Preserve the current collision penalties and release the waypoint when progress resumes: the best program’s three failures are timeouts, making route recovery more justified than broadly reducing caution.

3. **Separate stationary-law memory from uncertainty accumulated during observation gaps.** Test retaining slow-expert counts when no informative transition is available, while increasing predictive uniform mass with evidence age; keep the fast expert responsive when informative observations resume. Currently both count vectors decay on every enabled update, including skipped observations, so this could preserve useful stationary knowledge without assuming it remains reliable indefinitely.

4. **Preserve movement-expert identity across forecast steps.** Propagate uniform, slow, and fast laws separately through all three steps, combine their occupancy and collision predictions afterward, and retain the existing uniform floor and horizon-one behavior. This extends the best program’s successful ensemble by representing persistent uncertainty about the movement law, without introducing the deeper planning that has repeatedly weakened safety.

5. **Value safe continuation after intermediate targets.** Replace `value`’s immediate zero return at keys, doors, and frontiers with a short continuation that rewards target completion once and checks subsequent escape routes; keep actual escape terminal. This addresses a concrete blind spot in the otherwise successful three-step planner and could recover some of the efficiency seen in Generations 24 and 37 while retaining its immediate collision safeguards.
