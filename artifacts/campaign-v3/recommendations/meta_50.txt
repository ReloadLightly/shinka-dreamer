**Program Name: Online Occupancy Learning with Risk-Aware Dijkstra Planning**
- **Implementation**: Accumulates terrain and learns horizon-one enemy occupancy probabilities from anonymous local features using prior-smoothed counts, without forgetting older observations. Eight-direction Dijkstra planning combines movement, predicted risk and revisit costs, prioritizes keys and exit/door goals, explores frontiers, and waits when the next move is substantially riskier.
- **Performance**: Passed all validation tests with task score 0.799 across 72 development episodes, 77.8% escape, 20.8% death, 1.875 keys per episode, and 54.34 steps per successful escape.
- **Feedback**: Occupancy Brier losses modestly outperform supplied audit baselines, but sparse occupancy and absent comparative control results prevent attributing escape performance to learning. Stationary-regime escape is weakest at 70.8%; cumulative updates lack an explicit adaptation mechanism for switching dynamics, and only 14 switch episodes actually encountered a switch.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 46, `belief_route_survival`, remains the best program at 0.952483 fitness and 69/72 escapes.** Its combination of anonymous motion inference, multiple Dirichlet hypotheses, recovery waypoints and three-step survival planning outperforms Generation 48’s 0.916 and Generation 49’s 0.9298. These evaluations support the complete combination; they do not isolate individual components’ contributions.
- **Survival-conditioned planning recurs among strong programs.** Generations 42, 44 and 46 scored 0.9468, 0.9382 and 0.952483 respectively. Generations 48 and 49 retain three-step survival planning but achieve lower completion, reinforcing the earlier finding that the surrounding representation and routing matter.
- **Generation 49’s richer belief system improves on Generation 48’s reported results.** Censored occupancy likelihoods, restart hypotheses and separate adaptive/uniform concealed-enemy filters accompany one additional escape, one fewer timeout and a 0.0138 fitness gain. Successful escapes also shorten from 58.59 to 57.04 steps, although the simultaneous implementation differences prevent causal attribution.
- **Recovery-enabled routing remains part of successful combinations.** Generation 40 achieved 0.928 fitness with temporary waypoints; Generation 46 combines recovery with stronger inference and survival planning to reach 0.952483. Recovery also appears in Generations 48 and 49, showing that its presence alone does not guarantee leading performance.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Neither recent forecasting variant surpasses the current best.** Generation 48’s decayed, smoothed attempt counts produce 66 escapes, four deaths and two timeouts. Generation 49’s persistent concealed-enemy beliefs improve this to 67 escapes, four deaths and one timeout, but still trail Generation 46’s 69 escapes, two deaths and one timeout. The summaries do not identify which component caused the remaining failures.
- **Longer forecasts previously failed to deliver consistent gains.** Generation 38’s eight-step forecasts and undiscounted survival costs scored 0.787 with 13 deaths; Generation 41’s six-step configuration scored 0.9052. Both trail Generation 46’s three-step controller, without establishing horizon length alone as the cause.
- **Lower prediction loss does not consistently correspond to better control.** Generation 48’s near-cell Brier loss, 0.00680, improves on Generation 46’s 0.00711 despite substantially lower fitness. Generation 41 provides a stronger historical counterexample: 0.00542 near-cell loss but only 0.9052 fitness. Different visited states and sparse occupancy limit these comparisons.
- **Faster successful episodes do not compensate for additional failures.** Generations 48 and 49 finish successful episodes faster than Generation 46’s 59.20 steps, yet escape less often. This repeats the pattern from Generations 39 and 45, which averaged approximately 57.1 steps but achieved only 65/72 escapes and 0.9066 fitness.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program explicitly models attempted motion under anonymous, censored observations.** `_observed_destination` converts blocked and corner-blocked attempts into stays. `_observation_terms` restricts evidence to cells with previously visible predecessors and uses inclusion–exclusion to allow overlapping enemies. It also skips scheduled terrain-change boundaries and observed wall changes, reducing the risk of attributing terrain changes to movement dynamics.
- **Its learner preserves competing movement histories.** `_update_dynamics` maintains up to eight Dirichlet hypotheses, introduces restart mass according to observation informativeness, tempers likelihoods with exponent 0.85 and preserves the oldest surviving hypothesis. `_motion_law` blends these hypotheses with an explicit uniform component and a 10% uniform floor. This implementation accompanies 23/24 switching escapes, but adaptation’s causal contribution remains unmeasured.
- **Its planner propagates survival-conditioned enemy distributions.** `_survival_control` charges collision risk both before and after enemy movement, removes collision paths before subsequent projections, and represents hypothetical key collection and door opening. Beam selection preserves initial-action diversity and interaction states. These mechanics accompany two deaths and zero invalid actions in the best evaluation.
- **Persistent hidden-enemy filtering is not required for the strongest supplied result.** Generation 46 rebuilds a concealed-enemy density field in `_forecast`; its beam conditions visible-enemy distributions while using precomputed hidden hazards. Generation 49 adds persistent adaptive/uniform spatial filters but scores lower. Generation 46 also bypasses beam planning when no enemies are visible, accompanying approximately 0.70 reported candidate CPU seconds.

- **Generation 46 leads Generation 48 by approximately 0.0365 fitness and Generation 49 by 0.0227.** Their escape counts are 69, 66 and 67 respectively. Generation 49’s gain over Generation 48 comes with unchanged deaths and one fewer timeout; Generation 46’s advantage over Generation 49 includes two fewer deaths.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Preserve movement hypotheses through survival planning.** In `_survival_control`, propagate a small set of high-mass motion hypotheses separately, including the uniform component, and update their branch weights after conditioning on survival. Combine branch survival probabilities before taking the logarithmic risk penalty; this preserves persistent-law uncertainty that repeated propagation with `_motion_law`’s averaged kernel loses, extending the best program’s successful multi-hypothesis representation.

2. **Update direction beliefs according to what censored observations actually identify.** Within each dynamics hypothesis, evaluate `_evidence` on a compact weighted set of candidate motion laws and use likelihood-weighted posterior moments to update its representation. This can avoid excessive concentration from adding expected counts for ambiguous blocked attempts; retain restart hypotheses, the oldest surviving history and the uniform floor, which accompany the strongest supplied result.

3. **Add a safe-continuation value at the beam horizon.** For each retained third-step state, use its survival-conditioned enemy distributions to estimate the least collision risk among legal next actions and penalize states with no reasonably safe continuation. This extends the successful three-step controller without adopting the substantially longer tactical searches that performed less reliably, and could distinguish an apparently safe cul-de-sac from a position with an escape route.

4. **Make terrain refresh respond to blocked objectives.** When a known key, door or exit is unreachable under the remembered map, search a separate optimistic graph that treats stale interior walls as uncertain, then select a currently reachable observation position overlooking a promising blockage. Execute only moves legal under the current observed map; this builds on recovery waypoints and scheduled terrain awareness while addressing routes that ordinary distance-based recovery cannot discover.

5. **Give completed intermediate objectives a branch-specific continuation potential.** Replace `terminal = 0.0 if reached` with a cached next-objective value based on each branch’s collected keys, opened door and position, while keeping escape terminal. The current planner already models those interaction states, so this extends a demonstrated strength and may reduce wasted movement after collecting keys or reaching recovery waypoints without weakening its collision checks.
