**Program Name: Online Occupancy Learning with Risk-Aware Dijkstra Planning**
- **Implementation**: Accumulates terrain and learns horizon-one enemy occupancy probabilities from anonymous local features using prior-smoothed counts, without forgetting older observations. Eight-direction Dijkstra planning combines movement, predicted risk and revisit costs, prioritizes keys and exit/door goals, explores frontiers, and waits when the next move is substantially riskier.
- **Performance**: Passed all validation tests with task score 0.799 across 72 development episodes, 77.8% escape, 20.8% death, 1.875 keys per episode, and 54.34 steps per successful escape.
- **Feedback**: Occupancy Brier losses modestly outperform supplied audit baselines, but sparse occupancy and absent comparative control results prevent attributing escape performance to learning. Stationary-regime escape is weakest at 70.8%; cumulative updates lack an explicit adaptation mechanism for switching dynamics, and only 14 switch episodes actually encountered a switch.

# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 46, `belief_route_survival`, establishes the new best fitness: 0.952483**, surpassing the previous Generation 4 best of 0.950906. Both escaped in 69/72 episodes, but Generation 46 averaged 59.20 steps per escape versus 65.78. Its combination of anonymous motion inference, multiple Dirichlet hypotheses, route recovery, and survival-conditioned beam planning improves measured fitness and speed, with a safety tradeoff: two deaths and one timeout versus zero deaths and three timeouts.
- **Survival-conditioned planning succeeds in several configurations, but the surrounding implementation matters.** Generation 42 scored 0.9468 with 69 escapes and zero deaths; Generation 44 scored 0.9382 with 68 escapes and one death. Together with Generation 46, these results strengthen the earlier positive evidence from Generation 37’s belief-route controller, while preserving the previous finding that this planning approach is not uniformly beneficial.
- **Recovery-enabled routing accompanies strong completion and efficiency.** Generation 40’s temporary recovery waypoints achieved 0.928 fitness, 67/72 escapes, and 58.15 steps per escape. Generation 46 combines recovery with a richer learner and survival beam, reaching 69 escapes at 59.20 steps. These are successful combinations; the evaluations do not isolate recovery’s contribution.
- **Blocked-attempt and anonymous-observation modeling remain recurring features of strong programs.** Generation 46 extends the historical uniform/slow/fast pattern with an explicit uniform component and up to eight Dirichlet hypotheses. Generations 42 and 47 retain strong results with temporal experts, showing that multiple learning representations can support effective control.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Longer forecasts and temporal risk accumulation did not reliably improve outcomes.** Generation 38’s eight-step forecasts and undiscounted survival costs produced only 0.787 fitness, 55/72 escapes, and 13 deaths. Generation 41’s six-step forecasts and survival planning reached 0.9052, still below Generation 46’s three-step controller. These results extend the previous mixed evidence for deeper planning; they do not isolate horizon length as the cause.
- **Persistent expert identity did not establish an advantage over the best program’s blended motion law.** Generation 43 scored 0.912 with five deaths and 20/24 stationary escapes. Generation 47 improved to 0.9402 with zero deaths, but had four timeouts and 21/24 stationary escapes. Generation 46 instead uses a blended kernel and achieves 0.952483 with 22/24 stationary escapes.
- **Fast successful trajectories alone did not produce leading fitness.** Generations 39 and 45 both scored 0.9066 despite averaging approximately 57.1 steps per escape, faster than Generation 46. Each escaped in only 65/72 episodes. As in the earlier results, successful-episode duration excludes failures and cannot establish overall efficiency or reliability.
- **Better prediction diagnostics remain insufficient evidence of better control.** Generation 41’s near-cell Brier loss of 0.00542 is lower than Generation 46’s 0.00711, yet its fitness is 0.9052 rather than 0.952483. This reinforces the earlier Generation 6 counterexample. Sparse occupancy and different visited states complicate comparisons, and no supplied ablation establishes a causal learning benefit.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program explicitly handles anonymous, censored occupancy evidence.** `_observation_terms` restricts evidence to cells whose possible predecessors were previously visible, uses inclusion–exclusion to permit overlapping enemies, and skips scheduled terrain-change boundaries and observed wall changes. `_observed_destination` maps blocked and corner-blocked attempts back to their origin, preserving the distinction between attempted motion and realized displacement.
- **Its learner retains alternative movement histories without relying on only two decay rates.** `_update_dynamics` introduces fresh hypotheses at an information-scaled hazard, weights evidence by likelihood raised to 0.85, and preserves the oldest surviving hypothesis while retaining at most eight. Updates scale with observation informativeness; `_motion_law` adds a 10% uniform floor. This representation accompanies 23/24 switching escapes, although its causal contribution is unmeasured.
- **Its planner models collision survival separately from occupancy prediction.** `_survival_control` accounts for enemy presence both before and after movement, removes probability mass corresponding to collision paths, and carries surviving distributions forward. It excludes immediately occupied destinations and continues evaluating safety after reaching a navigation target. Hypothetical key collection and door opening are represented within branches.
- **Recovery and computation depend on the current situation.** `_select_route` detects idling, repeated positions, and stalled geometric progress, then selects temporary waypoints through low-risk routes. The beam preserves initial-action diversity and interaction states; when no enemies are visible, the controller takes a route step directly. These mechanisms accompany approximately 0.70 candidate CPU seconds in the reported timing metric and zero invalid actions.

## Performance Analysis

- **The new best improves fitness only slightly over the historical leader.** Generation 46 gains 0.001577 fitness and reduces successful escape duration by 6.58 steps, approximately 10%, while exchanging two timeouts for two deaths. It leads the supplied objective, but Generation 4 retains the stronger observed safety result.
- **Equal escape counts conceal substantial safety and speed differences.** Generation 42 also escaped 69/72 times, with zero deaths and three timeouts, but averaged 77.06 steps per escape and scored 0.9468. Generation 46 gains approximately 0.0057 fitness and finishes successful episodes 17.86 steps sooner, with two observed deaths.
- **Stationary dynamics remain the clearest regime weakness.** Generation 46 achieves 24/24 uniform, 22/24 stationary, and 23/24 switching escapes. Its stationary result matches Generations 4 and 42; Generations 43 and 47 achieve only 20/24 and 21/24. One episode changes a regime rate by 4.17 percentage points, so these rankings have limited resolution; paired outcome differences and uncertainty estimates are absent.
- **Prediction gains are stronger locally, while adaptation evidence remains limited.** Generation 46 improves near-cell Brier by approximately 27.9% over its supplied baseline, versus 6.6% for audit cells; threat loss improves approximately 35.3% over persistence. Post-switch near-cell loss is lower than pre-switch loss, but only 17 episodes encountered a switch, supplying 142 post-switch visible-enemy steps. These diagnostics support predictive accuracy without establishing adaptive learning or its contribution to escape performance.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Use arrival-time risk in route selection.** `_routes` currently applies horizon-one occupancy to every route edge; replace this with a few arrival-time bins, propagating the existing blended motion law and relaxing distant forecasts toward a background field. Preserve immediate occupied-cell exclusion, recovery waypoints, and the three-step survival controller: strong recovery results support improving route guidance, while longer tactical forecasts have produced mixed outcomes.

2. **Preserve distinct surviving enemy beliefs during beam pruning.** `_survival_control` merges branches with identical position, first action, and interaction state even when their surviving enemy distributions differ. Retain a small number of alternatives distinguished by conditional nearby collision risk, within the existing beam budget; this extends the best program’s survival conditioning by preserving histories that imply different safe continuations.

3. **Make hypothesis creation respond to predictive surprise.** Compare the observation likelihood under established hypotheses with that under a fresh prior, accumulate informative evidence of mismatch, and increase fresh-hypothesis mass when that evidence persists. Retain the oldest hypothesis, anonymous occupancy likelihood, and uniform floor; this builds on Generation 46’s successful learner while testing whether stationary knowledge can remain stable without slowing responses to switches.

4. **Carry concealed-enemy beliefs across observation gaps.** `_forecast` currently rebuilds hidden occupancy from a diffuse field each step; instead, propagate a compact spatial belief from previous observations, eliminate mass in visibly empty cells, and reconcile anonymous occupied cells without assigning permanent enemy identities. Preserve a diffuse component for unresolved enemies and reset affected terrain assumptions at change boundaries; this extends the best program’s hidden-enemy field and could improve risk estimates near recently disappeared threats.

5. **Give intermediate-target completion a branch-specific continuation value.** The current beam continues checking safety after reaching a target, but sets its navigation potential to zero thereafter; compute a lightweight next-objective or exploration potential using hypothetical collected keys and door state. Keep escape terminal and the three-step horizon unchanged, testing whether the successful survival planner can leave keys, doors, and recovery waypoints in a useful direction without sacrificing its collision safeguards.
