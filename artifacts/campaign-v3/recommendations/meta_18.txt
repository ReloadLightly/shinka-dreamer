# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 4, the current best, remains strongest at 0.9509 fitness:** 69/72 escapes, zero deaths, three timeouts, and 65.78 steps per successful escape. Its combination of uniform/slow/fast motion experts, anonymous transition inference, risk-weighted routing, and three-step planning outperformed every supplied alternative, including Generation 11’s **0.9300** and Generation 17’s **0.9250**. These comparisons support the combined design; they do not isolate individual components.
- **Motion learning coupled with explicit collision modeling remains a recurring successful pattern.** The earlier improvement from Generation 0’s cumulative occupancy learner (**0.799**) to Generation 1’s adaptive predictor (**0.917**) is reinforced by the strong ensemble-based Generations 4, 10 (**0.9138**), 11, and 17. Their implementations account for blocked attempts and ambiguous observations rather than interpreting observed displacement directly as attempted movement.
- **Recovery mechanisms coincide with improvements in some variants.** Generation 9’s stall recovery scored **0.8910**, versus Generation 8’s **0.8598**, while death fell from **15.3% to 8.3%**. Generation 11’s temporary recovery waypoints accompanied **93.1% escape and 2.8% death**. However, these programs also differ in learning and planning, and the best program achieves higher fitness without explicit recovery waypoints.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Greater planning depth and survival conditioning have not consistently improved outcomes.** Generation 6’s four-step survival-conditioned beam planner scored **0.7802**, with **15.3% timeouts** and **132.8 steps per escape**. Generation 12’s three-step survival-conditioned beam planner improved to **0.9066**, but still had **8.3% deaths**, versus zero for Generation 4. Conditioning itself is therefore not established as beneficial by these evaluations.
- **Additional model complexity has not reliably translated into better control.** Generation 2’s 113-hypothesis model and five-step planner scored **0.8703**. Generation 14’s expert-disagreement penalties scored **0.9030**, and Generation 15’s persistent hidden-enemy belief scored **0.9000**, with only **79.2% stationary escape**. These results show weaker completed designs, without establishing which added mechanism caused the deficit.
- **Faster successful escapes often accompanied worse reliability.** Generation 9 averaged **52.77 steps per escape** but escaped in **88.9%** of episodes; Generation 13 averaged **58.5 steps** with **91.7% escape**. Both trail Generation 4’s **95.8% escape** despite its longer successful trajectories. This extends the earlier Generation 5 pattern and remains subject to survivor selection: failed episodes are excluded from escape duration.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program infers attempted motion from anonymous occupancy explicitly.** `_terrain_destination` maps blocked and corner-blocked attempts back to their origin. `_infer_anonymous` sums assignments whose occupied-cell union matches observations, allowing enemy overlap. `_learn` discounts directionally uninformative posteriors and skips scheduled terrain-change boundaries. These mechanisms address concrete ambiguities in the task, although their individual performance contributions are unmeasured.
- **Generation 4 combines adaptation at two timescales with a persistent probability floor.** Slow and fast counts retain **0.985** and **0.86** of their excess over prior counts; likelihood-weighted experts include a fixed uniform model, and the final law reserves **12% uniform mass**. Its **23/24 switching escapes** are consistent with robust behavior, but do not establish that adaptive updates caused those escapes.
- **Its planner distinguishes occupancy prediction from collision hazard.** `_forecast` combines enemy presence before and after movement, subtracts the stay overlap, and incorporates diffuse unseen-enemy intensity. Planning excludes immediate destinations occupied by visible enemies and penalizes `-log(1-hazard)` over three steps, with future weights **1.0, 0.55, and 0.25**. These are concrete safety mechanisms associated with the strongest observed safety result.
- **Persistent mapping supports navigation, but timestamp storage does not make exploration responsive to stale terrain.** Displacement-based localization, frontier gain, and **18% target-retention tolerance** support consistent progress. However, `_gain` counts unseen cells rather than old observations. The best program’s **69.3% coverage**, **68.4% overall reconstruction**, and **98.5% stale-cell reconstruction** indicate that incomplete coverage remains a larger aggregate limitation than errors on previously observed terrain.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 4 exceeds the strongest new alternatives by 0.0209–0.0259 fitness.** Generations 11 and 17 both escaped in **67/72 episodes**, versus **69/72** for Generation 4. Generation 11 recorded two deaths and three timeouts; Generation 17 recorded four deaths and one timeout. Their successful escape durations—**66.13** and **65.49** steps—closely match the best program, so the clearest observed distinction is reliability.
- **Stationary dynamics remain the recurring weakness, but the best program handles them better.** Generation 4 escaped in **22/24 stationary episodes**, versus **21/24** for Generations 11 and 17, **20/24** for Generations 10, 12, 14, and 16, and **19/24** for Generation 15. Generation 15 nevertheless achieved **24/24 uniform escapes**, demonstrating that uniform-regime success does not imply comparable stationary reliability. Each regime contains only 24 episodes.
- **Prediction quality and task fitness do not rank programs consistently.** Generation 4’s near-cell Brier loss was **0.00761**, approximately **33% below** its supplied baseline. Generation 8 reported a lower absolute near-cell loss of **0.0060** but only **0.8598 fitness**; the earlier Generation 6 achieved **0.00293** with **0.7802 fitness**. Different visited states and sparse occupancy prevent these losses from establishing comparative control benefit.
- **Switching diagnostics remain limited and mixed.** Generation 4 encountered switches in **21 episodes**, contributing **701 post-switch steps**, including **340 with visible enemies**. Audit Brier decreased from **0.01577 to 0.01294**, while near-cell Brier increased from **0.00757 to 0.00787**. Generations 8 and 9 had only **39 and 58 post-switch visible-enemy steps**. These aggregates describe observed prediction behavior; without intervention results and uncertainty estimates, they do not establish a causal learning advantage.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add targeted observation goals for stale route bottlenecks.** Extend `_choose_target` to select reachable viewpoints that reveal old walls or diagonal corner cells separating the agent from a remembered door or exit, using `m["seen"]` and terrain-change boundaries. Activate this when objectives are unreachable or progress stalls; Generation 4’s three timeouts and two keys per episode make route recovery promising, while its high stale-cell accuracy argues against indiscriminate refreshing.

2. **Recover from repeated waits by committing briefly to a safe detour.** Track repeated position–target pairs and lack of route progress, then compare temporary waypoints that approach the objective through different corridors. Retain immediate visible-enemy exclusion and the three-step hazard planner, and abandon a waypoint if its risk rises; Generations 9 and 11 support testing bounded recovery, although neither establishes its isolated benefit.

3. **Make expert adaptation depend on informative predictive surprise.** Accumulate fast-versus-slow anonymous-observation log-likelihood evidence only on informative transitions, scaling contributions by observed enemy count and directional information. Sustained fast-expert advantage could introduce a fresh short-memory expert while preserving the slow model, uniform floor, and terrain-change exclusions; this extends the winning mixture without assuming that its mixed switching diagnostics already demonstrate successful adaptation.

4. **Separate persistent route obstruction from transient enemy danger.** In `_distances`, try discounting current occupancy risk with estimated arrival time, using short forecasts near the agent and a conservative longer-term risk estimate farther along the route. Keep immediate three-step collision costs unchanged: the current spatial potential applies horizon-one risk throughout a route, so this mutation tests whether transient threats cause unnecessary detours or waiting while preserving Generation 4’s strongest safety mechanisms.

5. **Carry door state through planning branches and enemy forecasts.** Replace `_forecast`’s unconditional assumed opening of adjacent doors with branch-specific transitions that match interaction timing, and carry the resulting door state through `_legal`, movement kernels, and planner memoization. This extends the best program’s explicit blocked-attempt modeling to a consequential terrain transition; because no supplied comparison isolates door-state forecasting, test it as a separate mutation while retaining the successful three-step horizon.
