# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 5, “Online Occupancy Learning with Risk-Aware Maze Planning,” is the strongest evaluated combination:** score **0.97**, **62/64 escapes**, two deaths, and **50.61 steps per escape**. It combines a ten-feature softmax enemy-motion model, anonymous occupancy gradients, persistent terrain memory, risk-weighted Dijkstra routing, and two-step hazard scoring. These results establish the combination’s performance; they do not isolate which component caused the gains.
- **Learned motion combined with efficient navigation appears across the leading programs.** Generation 2’s motion mixture scored **0.96**, with **61/64 escapes** and **59.28 mean steps**; Generation 9’s diffusion model with an online residual tree also scored **0.96**, with **61/64 escapes** and **56.43 steps per successful escape**. Distinct learned representations therefore achieved similar strong outcomes.
- **Threat forecasts consistently improved over persistence.** The best program’s threat Brier error was **0.02760 versus 0.04029**, a **31.5% reduction**. Generation 4’s geometry-constrained anonymous motion model achieved **0.0287 versus 0.0429** alongside a **0.95 score**, supporting anonymous occupancy prediction as a recurring successful component.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Adding refuge-risk reasoning did not consistently yield stronger survival.** Generation 3, “Adaptive Occupancy Flow with Risk-Aware Frontier Planning,” combined multiplicative motion-mixture updates with two-step refuge risk but scored **0.86**, with **16/64 deaths**. Generation 2 scored **0.96**, with three deaths. The summaries do not isolate the responsible change, but Generation 3’s improved forecasts over persistence were insufficient to prevent poor outcomes.
- **More elaborate motion inference was not uniformly competitive.** Generation 1’s eight-feature model with marginalized matchings, censored disappearances, and three-step planning scored **0.92**, with **55/64 escapes**. Generation 8’s contextual Bayesian censored-motion model scored **0.94**, with **60/64 escapes**, but required **107.02 steps per escape**—more than twice Generation 5’s **50.61**. Their additional inference machinery did not translate into the best measured outcome–efficiency combination.
- **Stronger reconstruction or lower threat error did not guarantee stronger escape performance.** Generation 1 reported **0.7974 unseen reconstruction**, yet trailed Generation 5, whose unseen reconstruction was **zero**. Generation 3’s threat Brier error of **0.0272** was slightly better than Generation 5’s **0.0276**, despite substantially worse survival. Neither metric alone explains controller quality.
## Implementation Insights
- **The best program learns directly from anonymous occupancy outcomes.** `_learn` differentiates the union-occupancy prediction through each source’s spatial softmax, avoiding persistent enemy identities. Its ten features represent staying, diagonals, approach direction, proximity, corridor geometry, and inferred flow. Prior shrinkage, accumulated squared-gradient scaling, and clipping constrain updates. This learner accompanies **0.00959 near-cell Brier error**, versus persistence’s **0.01399**.
- **Generation 5 separates route selection from immediate hazard assessment.** Dijkstra uses unit movement cost, local risk weights of **12 versus 4 farther away**, and visitation penalties. Action selection then assigns **180× immediate risk** and **75× continuation hazard**, including risk that a destination is occupied before the next move. Frontier information gain, target retention within **12%** of the best frontier score, and escalating waiting penalties provide explicit mechanisms for maintaining progress alongside caution.
- **Action conditioning is stronger in the exported forecast than in action comparison.** After selecting a move, `_record_forecast` predicts enemy motion relative to the proposed destination; that forecast is subsequently scored and learned from. During candidate comparison, however, immediate risk is shared across candidates, and the second prediction uses the current position. Thus the best program implements action-conditioned forecasting, but not a separately conditioned rollout for every candidate action.
## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 5’s lead is small in episode counts.** Its **0.97 score and 62/64 escapes** exceed Generations 2 and 9 by **0.01 score and one escape**, and Generation 4 by **0.02 score and three escapes**. These are development-set rankings; aggregate summaries provide no paired outcome breakdown establishing the statistical reliability of those differences.
- **Predictive gains are largest near hazards.** Generation 5 reduces Brier error relative to persistence by approximately **6.9% on audit cells**, **31.5% on near cells**, and **31.5% on threat cells**. Against the reported base forecast, improvements are smaller: **4.6% on audit cells** and **12.5% on near cells**. This supports localized forecasting value while distinguishing it from evidence that online learning caused additional escapes.
- **High aggregate model scores conceal substantial outcome variation.** Generation 0 scored **0.92** with model score **0.9887**, Generation 3 scored **0.86** with approximately **0.988**, and Generation 5 scored **0.97** with **0.98799**. All passed validation. The leading program’s advantage therefore lies in the evaluated interaction of representation, prediction, and control, rather than a uniquely high aggregate model score or validation success.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Condition hazard rollouts on each candidate action.** For every legal destination `q`, compute `risk_q = _predict(m, m["belief"], q)` and score immediate collision risk with `risk_q[q]`; for each continuation destination `r`, predict from `risk_q` conditioned on `r`, retaining the pre-move occupancy check. This extends Generation 5’s successful two-step planner by making action comparison consistent with its action-conditioned exported forecast; cache transition geometry to control computation.
2. **Preserve mutually exclusive enemy locations during belief propagation.** Replace repeated union propagation of fractional occupancy cells with anonymous source distributions: propagate each source’s possible locations categorically, then combine occupancy across sources. The current second prediction treats alternative locations as independent sources; preserving their relationship could improve continuation hazards while retaining the anonymous prediction approach shared by several strong programs.
3. **Add a regularized contextual residual to the successful softmax learner.** Keep the ten-feature model as the baseline and learn a small online residual tree over transition features such as proximity, corridor degree, inferred flow, and approach direction, adding its output to transition logits before normalization. Generation 9’s competitive residual-tree results support trying this hybrid; shrink residuals toward zero when observations are sparse so the existing model remains the default.
4. **Learn from the full observed transition overlap.** Replace `_learn`’s fixed previous-center 3×3 label window with the intersection of previous and current observed traversable terrain, retaining terrain-change exclusions and leaving unobserved destinations unlabeled. Differentiate the existing anonymous occupancy loss over this larger label set, normalizing gradients by usable labels so additional observations do not simply enlarge updates; this extends the learner associated with Generation 5’s strong near-cell and threat forecasts.
5. **Carry hazard reasoning through intermediate target arrival.** Replace unconditional `continuation = 0` at `q == target` with a short escape-option calculation for keys, doors, and frontiers, reserving zero continuation for a verified terminal exit. Retain Dijkstra routing, frontier hysteresis, and waiting penalties, but allow a slightly farther target when the nearest target has hazardous onward moves; this addresses a specific planning gap without replacing the efficient navigation that distinguishes Generation 5.
