# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 14, “Online Occupancy Learning with Survival-Conditioned Planning,” matches the strongest previous result:** **0.97 score, 62/64 escapes, two deaths, and no timeouts or invalid actions**. Its **50.34 steps per escape** is effectively tied with Generation 5’s **50.61**. Both combine anonymous occupancy learning, persistent terrain memory, risk-weighted Dijkstra routing, and short-horizon hazard assessment.
- **Preserving categorical movement alternatives accompanies a strong result in the current best.** Generation 14 retains each source’s mutually exclusive next positions and conditions continuation risk on surviving the first action. It achieves **five more escapes than Generation 12’s counterfactual flow planner** (**57/64; 0.93**) and one more than Generation 15 (**61/64; 0.96**). These program comparisons support the complete design, but do not isolate this mechanism’s causal contribution.
- **Multiple learned representations remain competitive.** Generation 10’s adaptive transport and residual tree achieved **0.96 and 61/64 escapes**, matching the escape count of earlier Generations 2 and 9. Generation 18’s hierarchical Dirichlet learner achieved **0.95, 60/64 escapes, and 51.27 steps per escape**, showing that strong outcomes are not exclusive to the softmax representation.
- **Forecast improvements near threats recur across successful programs.** Generation 14’s threat Brier error is **0.02774 versus 0.04111 for persistence**, a **32.5% reduction**, consistent with Generation 5’s earlier **31.5% reduction**. Generation 10 also improves audit error through its residual correction (**0.01647 versus 0.01709**), although neither comparison establishes additional escapes caused by online adaptation.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Generation 13’s route-consistent planning and Brier mixing traded low mortality for substantial failure to finish.** It scored **0.86**, with **48 escapes, three deaths, and 13 timeouts**; successful escapes required **86.17 steps**, versus Generation 14’s **50.34**. Its lower threat Brier error (**0.0256**) and higher coverage (**70.88%**) did not compensate for poor completion and efficiency.
- **Goal-aware calibration and survival rollouts were not consistently effective.** Generation 19 scored **0.84**, with **46 escapes and 18 deaths**, despite threat Brier error of **0.0277**, almost identical to Generation 14’s **0.02774**. Generations 16 and 17 also used survival-aware continuation reasoning but reached only **0.93** and **0.94**. The summaries do not identify which implementation differences caused these regressions.
- **More elaborate inference or deeper planning did not establish superiority.** Generation 18’s hierarchical matching, hidden tracking, and three-step planning remained below Generation 14 on escapes. This extends the earlier pattern from Generation 1 (**0.92**) and Generation 8 (**0.94; 107.02 steps per escape**): additional inference machinery can accompany competitive forecasts without delivering the strongest outcome–efficiency combination.
- **Refuge reasoning alone remains an unreliable indicator of survival.** Generation 11’s transport/refuge planner scored **0.95**, with five deaths, while earlier Generation 3 scored **0.86**, with 16 deaths. Both reinforce the previous finding that improved occupancy forecasts and explicit refuge scoring are insufficient to explain controller quality.
## Implementation Insights
- **The current best learns directly from anonymous union occupancy.** `_predict` combines source contributions as `1 − product(1 − contribution)`, and `_learn` differentiates that prediction through the observed sources’ spatial softmaxes. Its ten features encode staying, diagonals, approach, proximity, corridor geometry, and inferred flow. Prior shrinkage, gradient clipping, AdaGrad-style scaling, and bounded weights constrain adaptation without requiring persistent enemy identities.
- **Generation 14 preserves source alternatives through continuation evaluation.** `_continuation_risk` removes the first-action destination from each source’s possible positions and renormalizes by `1 − branch[survived]`. It combines discounted entry occupancy with subsequent movement risk before aggregating across sources. This retains categorical structure within each predicted source, although separate belief cells still act as independent sources and hidden-enemy identity uncertainty is not fully preserved.
- **Efficient navigation and strong local hazard penalties coexist in the best program.** Dijkstra charges unit movement cost, risk weights of **12 nearby versus 4 farther away**, and visitation penalties. Action selection adds **180× immediate risk** and **75× continuation hazard**. Frontier information gain, **12% target-retention tolerance**, and escalating waiting penalties provide concrete progress mechanisms consistent with its zero timeouts and approximately 50-step successful escapes.
- **Action conditioning remains asymmetric, extending the earlier Generation 5 observation.** First-step candidate risks come from a shared forecast centered on the current position; continuation transitions are conditioned on each candidate second destination. After selection, `_record_forecast` recomputes the exported and subsequently trained prediction around the chosen destination. Also, reaching **any selected target**, including a frontier or key, sets continuation cost to zero—not only reaching a terminal exit.
## Performance Analysis
- **Generation 14 is a joint development leader, not an established improvement over Generation 5.** Both score **0.97** with **62/64 escapes**. Generation 14 exceeds Generations 10 and 15 by one escape, Generation 18 by two, and Generation 11 by three. Aggregate results provide no paired disagreement counts or uncertainty estimates establishing reliable differences.
- **The largest forecast gains are localized near danger.** Generation 14 reduces Brier error against persistence by **6.7% on audit cells**, **32.5% on nearby cells**, and **32.5% on threat cells**. Against the reported base forecast, reductions are smaller: **4.6% on audit cells** and **11.8% nearby**. These distinguish predictive accuracy from evidence of an online-learning benefit.
- **Neither aggregate model quality nor threat error orders task performance reliably.** Generations 12, 14, and 19 all report model scores around **0.988**, yet their combined scores span **0.93, 0.97, and 0.84**. Generation 13 has lower threat error than Generation 14 but substantially worse completion. Forecast metrics also reflect each controller’s visited states, limiting direct interpretation across programs.
- **Spatial completeness remains distinct from escape success.** Generation 14 escapes in **96.875%** of episodes despite **62.84% coverage** and **zero unseen reconstruction**; visible reconstruction is **1.0** and stale reconstruction **0.986**. Generation 18 reports **92.43% terrain reconstruction** but fewer escapes. Together with the earlier results, this supports separating remembered-terrain accuracy, unseen reconstruction, predictive learning, and task outcomes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make first-step forecasts action-conditioned while retaining categorical continuation.** For each legal destination `q`, call `_predict(m, m["belief"], q)` and use both its occupancy at `q` and its source branches when evaluating continuation; pass these branches explicitly into `_continuation_risk`. Scope transition and inferred-flow caches to each candidate forecast, since branch-dependent velocities can differ between actions; this extends Generation 14’s successful planner and aligns action scoring with its exported forecast.
2. **Carry survival planning through intermediate targets.** Replace `q == target` as the condition for zero continuation with verified terminal-exit arrival; for keys, doors, and frontiers, evaluate onward moves using the inventory and door state that would result from the simulated action. Preserve Dijkstra routing, frontier hysteresis, and escalating waiting penalties, which accompany Generation 14’s zero timeouts, while removing its exemption from continuation hazards at intermediate goals.
3. **Preserve categorical uncertainty across observations, not just within a rollout.** Replace hidden-cell Bernoulli sources with weighted hypotheses over anonymous enemy-position sets, propagating legal movements and filtering against both observed enemies and observed empty cells. Aggregate occupancy only when exporting forecasts or computing route costs, while retaining the hypotheses for survival conditioning; this extends Generation 14’s successful categorical branches to address the independence approximation that currently reappears at every observation.
4. **Expand learning to the full reliable observation overlap.** Replace `_learn`’s previous-center 3×3 window with all traversable cells observed in both consecutive frames, excluding terrain changes and preserving unlabeled status outside that overlap. Normalize the occupancy-loss gradient by the usable label count before applying shrinkage and AdaGrad, retaining the anonymous union derivative and bounded weights associated with Generation 14’s strong threat forecasts while using more available supervision.
5. **Add a confidence-shrunk contextual residual to transition logits.** Keep the ten-feature softmax as the baseline and grow a small residual tree online from occupancy-loss gradients, allowing splits on proximity, corridor geometry, approach, and inferred flow; normalize the corrected logits through the existing spatial softmax. Shrink sparsely supported leaves toward zero and require sustained gradient evidence before splitting, drawing on Generation 10’s competitive transport/residual learner without assuming that its modest forecast improvement guarantees additional escapes.
