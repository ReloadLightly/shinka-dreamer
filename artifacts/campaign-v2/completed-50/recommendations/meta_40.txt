**Program Name: Online Occupancy Learning with Risk-Aware Dijkstra Planning**
- **Implementation**: Maintains a displacement-localized terrain map and learns prior-smoothed enemy occupancy probabilities from six local feature categories using successive observations. ## Implementation Insights
- **The best learns the quantity its planner consumes: anonymous union occupancy.** `_predict` combines source contributions through `1 − product(1 − contribution)`, and `_learn` differentiates occupancy Brier error through ten-feature spatial softmaxes without explicit identity matching. Prior shrinkage, gradient clipping, accumulated-gradient scaling, and bounded weights stabilize updates. Its nearby Brier error is **0.00957**, versus **0.01084** for the reported base and **0.01417** for persistence.
- **Its continuation calculation preserves categorical alternatives within each source.** `_continuation_risk` removes occupancy at the survived destination and renormalizes by `1 − branch[survived]`, then combines discounted entry hazard with subsequent movement risk. Generations 31, 36, and 37 share related survival-conditioned categorical reasoning and score **0.95–0.96**. However, the best treats hidden belief cells as separate sources, so it does not preserve all correlations between alternative locations of the same hidden enemy.
- **Hazard avoidance operates alongside explicit progress incentives.** The best uses Dijkstra risk weights of **12 nearby and 4 farther away**, plus **180× immediate risk** and **75× continuation hazard** in action scoring. Frontier information gain, target retention, visitation costs, and escalating waiting penalties accompany **zero timeouts and 50.34 steps per escape**. The timeout-heavy results from Generations 20 and 38 show why survival and progress must be assessed together, without isolating these incentives’ individual effects.
- **Two planning approximations remain consequential to interpretation.** Candidate first actions use a shared forecast centered on the current position; `_record_forecast` conditions the exported forecast on the selected destination only afterward. Also, reaching any selected target—including a key or frontier—sets continuation cost to zero. More explicitly action- or observation-conditioned variants in Generations 32–39 have not surpassed this implementation’s outcomes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Use action-conditioned forecasts for first-step scoring.** For each legal destination `q`, compute `_predict(m, m["belief"], q)` and pass its branches explicitly into `_continuation_risk`, with caches scoped to that candidate action. Retain the best program’s survival conditioning, two-step horizon, and progress penalties; this aligns action selection with the forecast subsequently exported and learned, while building on the 61-escape categorical planners.
2. **Preserve categorical uncertainty across hidden enemy locations.** Replace independent hidden-cell sources with persistent anonymous source distributions whose alternative locations propagate together, including an absence probability. Filter these distributions against visible empty cells and jointly associate occupied observations so one observation cannot confirm multiple sources; export union occupancy for Dijkstra and condition each entire source on survival for continuation planning. This extends the best’s successful categorical reasoning across observation gaps, where its current representation loses those correlations.
3. **Add a conservative contextual residual learner to the existing softmax.** Learn additive transition-logit corrections with a small online tree using corridor geometry, proximity, and inferred motion, driven by the existing anonymous occupancy-loss gradient. Require sustained gradient evidence before splitting and shrink low-support corrections toward zero, preserving normalized categorical transitions and the stabilized baseline updates. Generations 32–33 support this direction through roughly 17–18% nearby-error reductions, although their lower escape counts argue for retaining the leader’s planner initially.
4. **Continue safety planning through intermediate targets using simulated inventory.** Remove the unconditional `q == target` continuation exemption except when the simulated transition actually completes an exit, and compute second-step legality using the resulting keys and door state in a temporary planning state. After collecting a key or reaching a frontier, use a successor objective or frontier value so progress remains attractive while subsequent danger is charged. This preserves the short survival-conditioned planning seen in recent leaders while addressing a concrete blind spot without introducing the long, slow searches associated with timeout-heavy variants.
5. **Learn predictive freshness for remembered terrain and use it in routing.** Extend persistent map memory with per-cell change evidence and an age-dependent probability that remembered traversability still holds, updating those estimates when cells are reobserved. Add expected blockage or detour cost to Dijkstra and prioritize refresh viewpoints that resolve uncertainty along the current route, while preserving target retention, visitation costs, and escalating waiting penalties. This builds on the best’s successful map-based navigation and zero timeouts, adding learned treatment of dynamic terrain without assuming unsupported unseen-map reconstruction.
