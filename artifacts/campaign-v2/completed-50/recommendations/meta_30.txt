# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best remains the strongest outcome benchmark: 0.97, 62/64 escapes, two deaths, and no timeouts or invalid actions.** Its anonymous occupancy learning, persistent terrain memory, risk-weighted Dijkstra routing, and survival-conditioned two-step planning match the previously reported Generation 14 result and Generation 5’s escape count. None of Generations 20–29 exceeds it.
- **Joint enemy-state tracking with short recourse planning is competitive.** Generation 22, `joint_belief_recourse`, combines 64 joint particles, hierarchical motion learning, and 16 survival-conditioned scenarios. It achieves **0.96 and 61/64 escapes**, with **45.72 steps per escape versus the best’s 50.34**—approximately 9.2% fewer steps among successful episodes, although with one additional death.
- **Adaptive residual corrections repeatedly improve local predictions.** Generation 25 achieves **0.96 and 61/64 escapes**, reducing near-agent Brier error from **0.01098 to 0.00898** through its residual correction. Generation 24 reduces nearby error from **0.01288 to 0.01057**, while Generation 27 reduces it from **0.01073 to 0.00894**. These extend the earlier Generation 10 evidence for residual learning’s predictive usefulness; they do not establish additional escapes caused by adaptation.
- **Strong results span multiple representations.** Generation 22’s joint particles, Generation 25’s adaptive transport and residual tree, and the best program’s direct union-occupancy learner all reach **0.96–0.97**. Together with Generation 18’s **0.95** hierarchical Dirichlet result, this supports several viable representation–planner combinations rather than a uniquely superior learner.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Deeper planning and elaborate association do not consistently improve survival.** Generation 28, `context_transport_beam`, combines exact anonymous-association marginals with three-step beam planning but scores **0.89**, with **52 escapes and 12 deaths**. Its threat Brier error, **0.02758**, is slightly lower than the best’s **0.02774**, despite ten additional deaths. The summaries do not isolate which implementation difference causes this regression.
- **Low mortality can coexist with poor completion.** Generation 20’s hierarchical forecasting and four-step planner achieves **0.92**, with **55 escapes, three deaths, and six timeouts**; successful escapes require **74.75 steps**. Its **72.18% coverage** and **0.0252 threat Brier error** exceed the best on exploration extent and threat prediction, respectively, without matching task performance. This repeats Generation 13’s earlier timeout-heavy pattern.
- **Additional learning and routing machinery does not reliably surpass the best.** Generations 26 and 27 introduce overlap-oriented learning, key ordering, and survival planning, yet both score **0.95**, with **59 escapes and five deaths**. Generation 29’s action-conditioned transport planner reaches **0.94**, with **58 escapes and six deaths**. These complete designs underperform the best; the available comparisons cannot attribute the losses to individual mechanisms.
- **Generation 21 provides no usable evidence of algorithmic effectiveness.** Its occupancy propagation, logistic corrections, and state-expanded Dijkstra planner receive **0.0 and fail validation**. Because no failing test or diagnostic is supplied, the failure cannot be assigned to its representation, learning rule, or navigation logic.
## Implementation Insights
- **The best directly optimizes anonymous union occupancy.** `_predict` aggregates source contributions as `1 − product(1 − contribution)`. `_learn` differentiates observed occupancy error through visible-source spatial softmaxes using ten motion features, prior shrinkage, gradient clipping, accumulated-gradient scaling, and bounded weights. This avoids explicit identity matching while supporting **0.00957 nearby Brier error**, versus **0.01417 for persistence**.
- **Its continuation calculation preserves mutually exclusive alternatives within each source.** `_continuation_risk` excludes occupancy at the survived first destination and renormalizes by `1 − branch[survived]`. It combines discounted entry hazard with subsequent movement risk before aggregating across sources. This is a concrete strength of the best’s short-horizon planner, although independence between belief-cell sources still approximates hidden-enemy uncertainty; Generation 22 instead represents joint configurations explicitly.
- **Progress incentives accompany strong hazard avoidance.** The best’s Dijkstra costs weight nearby risk by **12** and farther risk by **4**; action selection adds **180× immediate risk** and **75× continuation hazard**. Frontier information gain, **12% target-retention tolerance**, visitation penalties, and escalating waiting costs coexist with zero timeouts and approximately **50 steps per escape**. Their individual contributions are not isolated.
- **The best retains two consequential planning approximations.** Candidate first actions share a forecast centered on the current position; only after selection does `_record_forecast` recompute the forecast around the chosen destination. Also, reaching **any selected target** sets continuation cost to zero, including keys and frontiers. These qualify the interpretation of its action conditioning and survival horizon despite its leading outcomes.
## Performance Analysis
- **The latest evaluations approach but do not improve the outcome leader.** Against the best’s **62/64 escapes**, Generations 22 and 25 achieve **61**, Generation 24 achieves **60**, and Generations 23, 26, and 27 achieve **59**. These small development-set differences lack paired disagreement counts and uncertainty estimates establishing reliable superiority.
- **Forecast rankings do not determine escape rankings.** Generation 27’s nearby Brier error of **0.00894** beats the best’s **0.00957**, but it has three more deaths. Generation 20 has lower threat error yet six timeouts; Generation 28 has nearly identical threat error yet substantially more deaths. This reinforces the earlier Generation 19 result: predictive accuracy and controller effectiveness are distinct measurements, also influenced by the states each policy visits.
- **The best’s predictive gains are concentrated near danger.** Relative to persistence, its Brier reductions are approximately **6.7% overall, 32.5% nearby, and 32.5% on threats**. Against the reported base forecast, improvements are smaller: **4.6% overall and 11.8% nearby**. These support forecast quality without establishing the causal effect of online updates on escape success.
- **Map completeness remains separate from task success and predictive learning.** The best escapes in **96.875%** of episodes with **62.84% coverage**, perfect visible reconstruction, **98.60% stale reconstruction**, and zero unseen reconstruction. Generation 20 explores more but finishes less often; Generation 25 reaches **61 escapes with 61.9% coverage**. Higher coverage alone is therefore not evidence of a better controller or stronger learned dynamics.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Score each first action with its own action-conditioned forecast.** For every legal destination `q`, call `_predict(m, m["belief"], q)` and pass the returned source branches explicitly into `_continuation_risk`, using a separate continuation cache for each candidate. Preserve the best program’s categorical survival conditioning and two-step horizon; this removes the mismatch between the forecast used to choose an action and the forecast subsequently exported and trained.
2. **Continue survival planning beyond keys and frontiers.** Replace the `q == target` continuation exemption with a verified terminal-exit condition, and evaluate second moves using the inventory and door state produced by the simulated action. Retain frontier hysteresis, visitation penalties, and escalating waiting costs, which accompany the best’s zero timeouts; intermediate progress should still account for the danger immediately afterward.
3. **Use joint enemy hypotheses beneath the existing short-horizon planner.** Maintain approximately 64 weighted particles over anonymous enemy configurations, propagate legal motion, and filter against observed occupied and empty cells; condition whole configurations on survival before evaluating second-step recourse. Generation 22’s 61 escapes and shorter successful routes justify this variation: export marginal occupancy for the existing Dijkstra costs while preserving joint uncertainty for local action selection.
4. **Add a confidence-shrunk residual tree to the successful transition learner.** Keep the ten-feature softmax baseline and learn additive logit corrections from anonymous occupancy-loss gradients, with contextual splits on proximity, corridor geometry, and inferred motion. Require sustained gradient evidence before splitting and shrink low-support leaves toward zero; Generations 24, 25, and 27 support residual adaptation’s predictive potential, while spatial softmax normalization preserves categorical movement alternatives.
5. **Expand reliable supervision while preserving the anonymous union-occupancy objective.** Train on unchanged traversable cells visible in both consecutive observations, rather than only the previous center’s 3×3 neighborhood, and compute gradients through the saved pre-observation forecast without labeling unseen cells as empty. Average data gradients over usable labels before applying separately scaled prior shrinkage and AdaGrad, retaining bounded weights; this extends the best’s successful learning mechanism, although Generations 26–27 caution that broader supervision alone need not improve escapes.
